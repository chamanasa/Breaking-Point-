Introduction

A structured way to size anything, from chai stalls to smartphone sales, in fifteen minutes and out loud.

What is a guesstimate?

A guesstimate asks you to estimate a number you cannot possibly know, such as the cars sold in India each year or the daily revenue of a petrol pump. You work it out from a handful of reasonable assumptions and some quick arithmetic.

Why interviewers ask them

They are testing how you think, not what you know: whether you can break down an ambiguous problem, make sensible assumptions, calculate quickly and explain yourself clearly under pressure.

What the interviewer is scoring

How close is close enough?

Being in the right ballpark matters more than precision. An answer anywhere from half to double the true figure, reached through clean logic, is a strong answer.

The five steps

Here is the method applied to one question: “How many new cars are sold in India each year?”

StepWhat you doOn this question
1 ClarifyFix geography, time period, units and scope.New passenger cars only, all of India, one year, units not value.
2 ApproachPick demand-side or supply-side and say why.Demand-side from households, since ownership is driven by income.
3 SegmentBuild a MECE issue tree.Urban vs rural, then high, middle and low income.
4 EstimateAssign a value to each branch and calculate.Ownership share × replacement every ~8 years, plus first-time buyers.
5 ValidateCross-check with another method or a benchmark.Compare with reported sales of ~4 M a year and explain any gap.

Step 3 up close: the issue tree

Segmentation is where most answers are won or lost. Draw the tree before any numbers go in, and check that each level splits cleanly, with no overlaps and no gaps.

Before calculating, walk the interviewer through your tree and ask whether the structure makes sense. Fixing it now costs seconds; fixing it later costs the case.
